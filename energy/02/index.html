44 of this week's 75 Indian filings came from a university

A patent office is a register of who thought something was worth protecting. Among Indian applicants this week the answer was an academic institution 59 times in a hundred, which is 4 points below the 63 per cent the Indian record has run at across the financial year. Indian companies filed 15 this week, named individuals 16.

Academic filings are a real signal of what problems are being worked on, but a patent filed by a college is not a company, and the gap between the two columns is the gap a venture investor is looking at.

Grid & power networks carried the week, on 26 filings

This week the Indian weight sat in grid & power networks, with 26 of the 75 filings from Indian applicants. Batteries & fuel cells followed on 9. About this report
What it counts, and what it does not

WHAT THIS COUNTS. Patent applications published by the official registers of India and Europe, identified as energy by their official technology classification, and counted from the primary records rather than a commercial database. Every applicant is read as a company, a university or public body, or a named individual, and the three are always reported separately. A total that blends ninety college filings with ten corporate ones tells you nothing.

WHAT IS EXCLUDED. Vehicle integration. Battery packs, mounting, enclosures and vehicle thermal management. Charging infrastructure and connectors. Drivetrain, traction motors and regenerative braking. Vehicle side battery management. The test we apply is simple: Would this patent still matter if no vehicle existed? Borderline records are decided against inclusion rather than for it.

WHAT WE DO NOT CLAIM. Weekly counts in a sector this size are small. We test every part of the sector against its own history before calling anything a movement, and in most weeks the honest answer is that nothing moved. We print that answer rather than dressing a difference of four filings as a trend.

WHAT THESE NUMBERS CANNOT TELL YOU. Applicant origin is the address on the published record, not corporate ownership, so a locally registered subsidiary of a foreign group reads as domestic. Inventor residence is a residence field, not a nationality field. First appearance means first appearance in this financial year, not an applicant's first ever filing.

The two registers do not describe their records to the same depth. India publishes an ordered classification, so we can tell a record's main subject matter from a secondary one. Europe does not, so the European filter is the looser of the two and the European count is the more generous. Read every India against Europe comparison in that light.

Publication is the observable weekly event. The date an application was filed is a property of the record, not something that happened this week. And a patent is evidence of an intention to protect something, not of a working product, revenue, or a company.

CORRECTIONS. If you find an error, tell us, and the next issue will say so. Startt does not publish the classification rules behind this report, but every figure in it can be traced to a table in the accompanying data file.
