India  /  Financial year to date, week of 24/07/2026

India's energy patents come from campuses, not companies.

Of the 1,255 energy patent applications published by Indian applicants this financial year, 795 came from universities and public bodies and 173 from companies. One foreign filer, LG Energy Solution, Ltd., filed 185 in by itself, more than every Indian company in the sector managed between them.

The largest Indian corporate filer, Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Limited, published 7 so far this financial year.

Who is doing the work
Section One

Indian applicants only, 1,255 filings. Filings from abroad are counted separately in Section Three. A further 68 records carry no usable address country and are excluded from this cut.

63 per cent of the Indian record comes from a university

A patent office is a register of who thought something was worth protecting. In this sector the answer is overwhelmingly an academic institution. Universities and public bodies account for 795 of the 1,255 filings by Indian applicants. Companies account for 173. Named individuals, mostly academics filing personally, account for 287.

The corporate list is short, and it is incumbents rather than new entrants. Hindustan Petroleum Corporation Limited leads with 7. The specialists a venture investor would expect to see appear once or twice each near the bottom rather than at the top.

This is not a criticism of the universities. It is a description of where the risk is currently being carried. Academic filings are a real signal of what problems are being worked on. But a patent filed by a college is not a company, and the gap between the two columns is the gap a venture investor is looking at.

Campuses out file companies in every category with real volume

Split the sector by who filed and the same pattern repeats in every row. Across the 16 categories with enough volume to read, Synthetic fuels has the highest corporate share at 37 per cent, and even there companies file 10 against 11 from universities. In grid & power networks, universities out file companies by 303 to 37.

Where corporate filings cluster, someone is already paying for the market. Where academic filings cluster and corporate ones do not, one of two things is true: the technology has not found a business model yet, or nobody has built the company.

Which of those it is cannot be read off a patent count. What the count does say is where to look.

More European filings come from Korea than anywhere else.

Applicants based in Korea published 2,212 energy filings in Europe this financial year, against 1,655 for Germany, France and the United Kingdom combined. Europe publishes 4.3 times India's volume, and 94 per cent of it comes from companies.

Universities account for 4 per cent here. In India, counting all applicants the same way, the figure is 37 per cent, and counting only Indian applicants it is 63 per cent.

About this report
What it counts, and what it does not

WHAT THIS COUNTS. Patent applications published by the official registers of India and Europe, identified as energy by their official technology classification, and counted from the primary records rather than a commercial database. Every applicant is read as a company, a university or public body, or a named individual, and the three are always reported separately. A total that blends ninety college filings with ten corporate ones tells you nothing.

WHAT IS EXCLUDED. Vehicle integration. Battery packs, mounting, enclosures and vehicle thermal management. Charging infrastructure and connectors. Drivetrain, traction motors and regenerative braking. Vehicle side battery management. The test we apply is simple: Would this patent still matter if no vehicle existed? Borderline records are decided against inclusion rather than for it.

WHAT WE DO NOT CLAIM. Weekly counts in a sector this size are small. We test every part of the sector against its own history before calling anything a movement, and in most weeks the honest answer is that nothing moved. We print that answer rather than dressing a difference of four filings as a trend.

WHAT THESE NUMBERS CANNOT TELL YOU. Applicant origin is the address on the published record, not corporate ownership, so a locally registered subsidiary of a foreign group reads as domestic. Inventor residence is a residence field, not a nationality field. First appearance means first appearance in this financial year, not an applicant's first ever filing.

Publication is the observable weekly event. The date an application was filed is a property of the record, not something that happened this week. And a patent is evidence of an intention to protect something, not of a working product, revenue, or a company.

CORRECTIONS. If you find an error, tell us, and the next issue will say so. Startt does not publish the classification rules behind this report, but every figure in it can be traced to a table in the accompanying data file.
